### 2.2 Government notices and project filings: discover real consumers, new supply, and input requirements

**Access:** Public web search, readable pages, and original documents where accessible.

Government project filings are particularly useful for physical chemical trading. **Environmental impact assessment (EIA) reports** frequently describe processes, equipment, nameplate capacity, raw materials, and annual material consumption. Those details are much stronger consumption evidence than a business registration entry.

| Source | Information to look for | Sales and procurement value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ministry of Ecology and Environment, provincial and municipal environmental agencies** | EIA acceptance, proposed approvals, approvals, reports, completion acceptance, public environmental disclosures | Find new/expanded factories; identify processes, inputs, annual consumption, site, and project status. |
| [**National Pollutant Discharge Permit Information Platform**](https://permit.mee.gov.cn/) | Public permit and operating-entity information | Support checks on production location and activity; a permit entry alone does not prove capacity for a specific product. |
| **NDRC, local approval bureaus, government-service portals, investment-project filing notices** | Project registration, approval, energy reviews, planned investment, and construction scope | Discover potential capacity additions and track progress. |
| **Industry/IT authorities, chemical parks, and industrial-zone websites** | Major projects, sector plans, resident companies, capacity-replacement and technical-upgrade notices | Identify clustered buyers, operating plants, and future supply. |
| **Emergency-management authorities and the [National Hazardous Chemicals Safety Public Service Platform](https://whpdj.mem.gov.cn/publicInternet/home)** | Hazardous-chemical registrations and relevant safety licensing disclosures | Help verify regulated status and license scope; always check the competent authority's current records. |

**Search for production processes and equipment, not just product names.** When sourcing SBS consumers, queries such as “modified asphalt EIA SBS annual consumption,” “waterproofing membrane modifier raw-material table,” or the corresponding Chinese process terms may uncover facilities whose project titles do not mention SBS at all.

> **Task:** We sell SBS and want to find new, expanded, or retrofitted modified-asphalt and waterproofing-membrane plants in eastern China, prioritizing projects with explicit SBS input requirements.
>
> **Sources and methods:** Use downstream customer-development skills. Start with relevant industries and companies in Chemcloud Data Hub, then read EIA reports and raw-material tables from local environmental agencies, industrial parks, and development/reform authorities. Cross-check factory sites and project-acceptance records. Search both “SBS” and Chinese process terms such as “改性剂,” “胶体磨,” and “发育罐.”
>
> **Deliverables:** A project-level Excel database and regional project map, including legal project owner, plant, process, annual inputs, stage, original PDF/page, evidence grade, and recommended contact timing. Show an inline timeline; separate projects without direct consumption evidence.

**Keep project stages distinct:** an EIA acceptance, proposed approval, or approval is not proof that construction has begun. A projected startup is not the same as an operating plant. ChemClaw's customer-development skills distinguish project evidence from confirmed current purchases.

### 2.12 Why the workflow matters more than a list of websites

ChemClaw includes business skills for prospecting, expansion-project research, supplier verification, quote comparison, and market studies. Those skills are designed to **select sources by task and distinguish search leads from verified evidence**.

A typical workflow looks like this:

1. **Narrow the scope using industry data and task context.** Domestic spot work starts with Chemcloud Data Hub; overseas work defines product, country, and counterparties; futures research defines contracts, frequency, and indicators.
2. **Corroborate using original sources.** Check corporate sites, EIAs, public filings, licensing platforms, exchanges, tenders, overseas company materials, and energy reports rather than relying only on search-result snippets.
3. **Turn documents into business judgments or testable questions.** Is annual feedstock usage disclosed? Has the new plant started? Is the overseas inquiry legitimate? Are prices comparable? For quant research, were data actually public at the alleged signal time, and were contracts tradable?
4. **Deliver usable, auditable outputs.** Customer development trackers, domestic/international supplier RFQs, project pipeline registers, physical-price comparison sheets, basis reports, and backtest workbooks—with citations and unresolved questions.

**For customer leads:** Why might this company buy my product? Which document supports it? When should I contact them?

**For suppliers:** Why can this firm supply the right product? Is it a plant or a channel? Which qualification, quality, and delivery questions remain?

**For prices:** Which day, market, grade, and delivery/payment basis does this figure represent? Is it genuinely comparable with the price in front of me?

**Availability:** Built-in tools cannot guarantee third-party uptime; public search does not confer access to paid subscriptions. Some websites need logins, captchas, or human takeover; some PDFs are scanned images. Cite original records where accessible, identify missing evidence where not, and **never confuse knowing a website exists with having read and verified its contents**.

| Topic | Practical question | Key calculation or convention |
| --- | --- | --- |

### Quantitative research: move from “looks correlated” to a defensible result

ChemClaw has skills for **price/inventory seasonality, indicator correlations, lead–lag relationships, inventory-price and utilization-price studies, extreme basis/calendar-spread scans, shutdown and capacity-expansion event studies, historical analogue matching, and explicit-rule backtesting**.

Two recurring traps are **using data that were not yet published at the historical decision time** and **treating a statistical correlation as a tradable rule**. The research skills address these directly:

- **Information-availability time:** Weekly inventories have observation periods and actual publication timestamps. Signals must use only information available when the hypothetical decision was made.
- **Frequencies and gaps:** Inventory/utilization may be weekly, futures daily. Missing values cannot be fabricated or zero-filled.
- **Statistics:** Lag scans report sample size, effect strength, significance, and multiple-testing adjustments; results are rechecked by year and regime.
- **Event studies:** Use first disclosure times for outages, startups, and policy actions, and account for other simultaneous market drivers.
- **Backtests:** Translate “buy when inventories fall and basis is strong” into an explicit rule, align signal times, and model rolls, fees, slippage, and limits using **real listed futures contracts**, plus out-of-sample and sensitivity tests. A back-adjusted continuous price is not an executable contract.

> **Task:** Test whether Chinese methanol port-inventory changes consistently lead MA futures returns by 1–8 weeks in recent years.
>
> **Sources and methods:** Use licensed inventory observations with genuine initial release timestamps (Chemcloud Data Hub, industry records, or my uploads); fetch futures through `chem_futures_history`. Apply lead–lag research methods, distinguishing inventory levels from changes and release dates from statistical reference dates; check stationarity, frequencies, gaps, and multiple-testing adjustments across 25 lags.
>
> **Deliverables:** A filterable interactive report (lag coefficients, scatterplots, rolling correlation, annual/regime results) and recalculable Excel (sample size, coefficients, p-values, FDR-corrected q-values, date alignment). Do not present statistical lead–lag as a reliable tradable forecast.

> **Task:** Backtest this methanol futures research rule: if port inventory has fallen for two consecutive weeks and basis exceeds its rolling three-year 80th percentile, open a position the next tradable day and hold for ten trading days.
>
> **Sources and methods:** First use the rule-backtesting skill to formalize the signal, observation and trade times, entry/exit, contract roll, costs, and position limits **for my approval before execution**. Obtain genuine published inventory and basis data from Chemcloud Data Hub, licensed sources, or uploads, checking real publication timestamps. Use verified daily prices and ZCE contract data; do not use adjusted continuous historical prices as directly executable fills.
>
> **Deliverables:** After the rule is confirmed, produce Excel trades, returns, costs, drawdowns, rolls, and out-of-sample tests, plus an interactive equity-curve/drawdown/sensitivity dashboard and time-availability audit. Disclose inadequate data; never invent returns or place real trades.

## Before you use ChemClaw

- **A decision-support tool, not the final decision-maker.** Verify important trading, procurement-contract, product-quality, and import/export compliance matters against primary sources.
- **Not a trading engine or plant-control system.** ChemClaw supports research, sourcing, analysis, and document work; it does not autonomously execute futures trades or control production equipment.
- **Open source:** Apache-2.0. Built on Fei2411's DS Bot and DeepSeek Harness; see the repository's `NOTICE` for acknowledgments and license notices.
