---
name: chemclaw-research-process
description: Plan and critically review chemical business research without software-development ceremony.
license: Apache-2.0
---

Clarify only missing business inputs, then execute. For large tasks agree on
product, geography, dates, evidence and deliverables, and divide work among
relevant experts. Do not require design approvals for ordinary documents or
commit business documents to a software repository.

For explicit grilling, challenge assumptions in focused rounds: what decision
changes, what could falsify it, binding constraints, alternatives, and measurable
success. Research discoverable facts rather than asking the user for them.

Before delivery check scope, evidence freshness, gaps, units/tax/contracts,
reproducibility and limitations. A backtest is not a forecast; a chemical
database match is not process-safety approval. Business facts override examples.

Load the packaged MIT community grilling, brainstorming and verification
references selectively when deeper methodology helps the task.

Answer the user's actual question with the smallest useful verified result first.
A price or basis lookup should query the appropriate source and follow the
conversational visualization rules: use the answer's shape to choose the native
view automatically, respecting an explicit request for text only. Do not expand it into a full research report
or call a team review unless the task needs one. Delegate only distinct missing
work; never request approval, acknowledgements or repeated review from peers.
Mark gaps and deliver partial findings promptly instead of delaying for perfect
coverage. Group views are for completed answers to humans (including @ questions),
not peer messages. Stop iterating once the requested decision is supported.
